# SOP-16 High discharge COD and biological upset

Area: 300 and 500. Tags: AIT-502, AIT-301, SC-301, AIT-401.

## 16.1 Purpose and scope
COD (chemical oxygen demand) measures the organic load left in the treated effluent. The aeration basin removes
dissolved COD biologically; the clarifier removes the particulate part. The consent limit is 250 mg/L, with a
pre-alarm at 220 mg/L.

## 16.2 Symptoms and alarms
- Discharge COD high (AIT-502 HI), above consent (AIT-502 HIHI).
- Low dissolved oxygen in the aeration basin, or the blower at full speed for long periods.
- Foaming or a change in sludge colour in the aeration basin.

## 16.3 Likely causes
- A high organic load from the dye-house (SOP-01).
- Insufficient aeration: blower trip or low dissolved oxygen (SOP-06).
- High clarified turbidity carrying particulate COD (SOP-09).
- Toxic or very hot discharges inhibiting the biology (SOP-12).

## 16.4 Immediate checks
- Check the dissolved oxygen trend and the blower status.
- Check the clarified turbidity.
- Take a grab sample for a laboratory COD to confirm the analyser.
- Ask the dye-house about unusual chemicals or recipes in the last 12 hours.

## 16.5 Corrective actions
- Restore aeration first (SOP-06).
- Reduce the load to the aeration basin using the equalisation storage.
- Ask the dye-house to hold concentrated baths.

## 16.6 Escalation and records
- If AIT-502 exceeds 250 mg/L, follow SOP-10.
- Record the laboratory COD and the actions.

# SOP-06 Aeration blower trip and low dissolved oxygen

Area: 300 Aeration. Tags: B-301_CMD, B-301_RUN, SC-301, JT-301, AIT-301.

## 6.1 Purpose and scope
Blower B-301 supplies air to the aeration basin, where bacteria remove the dissolved organic load (COD).
Dissolved oxygen (DO, AIT-301) is normally controlled at about 2 mg/L by the blower speed. Without air the DO
falls to zero within minutes, and after an hour or more the removal of COD deteriorates.

## 6.2 Symptoms and alarms
- Discrepancy alarm: blower B-301 commanded on but not running.
- Blower power JT-301 drops to zero while the speed command SC-301 is still high.
- Dissolved oxygen falling fast, then DO low and DO very low (AIT-301 LO, LOLO).
- Later: discharge COD (AIT-502) rising.

## 6.3 Likely causes
- Blower motor overload trip, VFD fault, or loss of the motor supply.
- High discharge pressure (diffuser fouling) or a closed air valve.
- Belt failure, or a high bearing temperature shutdown.

## 6.4 Immediate checks
- Check the VFD display and the motor control centre for the trip reason.
- Check the blower discharge pressure and the air valve positions.
- Look and listen for belt or bearing damage before any restart.
- If the blower is actually running, confirm the DO probe reading with the portable DO meter.

## 6.5 Corrective actions
- Ask the control room to start the standby blower if one is available, then investigate the tripped unit.
- Reset and restart the blower only once the trip cause is understood and cleared.
- If the blower cannot be restored within 2 hours, reduce the forward flow to the aeration basin using the
  equalisation storage and inform the supervisor.

## 6.6 Escalation and records
- Call the electrical technician for VFD or motor faults.
- Record the trip time, the cause, the minimum DO and the time back in service.

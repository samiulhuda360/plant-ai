# SOP-13 Dye-house dispensing station: chemical tank low and batch hold

Area: 900 Dye-house chemical dispensing. Tags: LIT-901, LIT-902, LIT-903, DS-900, FIT-901, FQ-901.

## 13.1 Purpose and scope
The automatic dispensing station doses soda ash solution (LIT-901), salt brine (LIT-902) and acetic acid
(LIT-903) to the dyeing machines by recipe. Each tank is made up automatically from the dissolving system when
it falls below the make-up point. If a tank runs low the station holds new batches, which stops production.

## 13.2 Symptoms and alarms
- Rate-of-change alarm: soda ash, salt brine or acetic acid tank level falling fast.
- Tank level low (LIT-901 LO, LIT-902 LO or LIT-903 LO).
- Station state changes to hold (DS-900 = 2) and the batch counter FQ-901 stops increasing.

## 13.3 Likely causes
- Dissolving system fault: soda ash or salt silo empty, feeder blocked, or make-up water valve closed.
- A drain or flush valve on the tank left open or passing.
- Level transmitter fault.

## 13.4 Immediate checks
- Compare the level transmitter with the tank sight glass.
- Walk the tank bund for an open drain valve or leak.
- Check the silo level, the screw feeder and the make-up water valve of the dissolving system.
- Check the solution strength with the conductivity or density check before batches resume.

## 13.5 Corrective actions
- Close any passing drain valve.
- Restore the make-up; if the dissolver has failed, prepare a manual batch of solution to the recipe strength.
- Tell the dye-house planner about the hold so machines can be resequenced.

## 13.6 Escalation and records
- Inform the dye-house supervisor of any hold longer than 15 minutes.
- Record the cause and the time the station was on hold.

# SOP-05 Chemical day tank low level and make-up failure

Area: 200 Chemical storage. Tags: LIT-201, LIT-202, FIT-202, P-201_SP, P-202_SP.

## 5.1 Purpose and scope
The acid (LIT-201) and coagulant (LIT-202) day tanks are refilled automatically from bulk storage when they fall
below the make-up point. If the bulk container is empty, the transfer pump fails, or a drain valve is left
passing, the day tank runs low and the dosing pump will eventually run dry and stop dosing.

## 5.2 Symptoms and alarms
- Rate-of-change alarm: day tank level falling fast, faster than the dosing rate explains.
- Level low (LIT-201 LO or LIT-202 LO), then coagulant day tank nearly empty (LIT-202 LOLO).
- The level drops below the make-up point and does not rise.
- Once the tank is empty: dosing flow FIT-202 at zero and clarified turbidity rising.

## 5.3 Likely causes
- Bulk container or IBC empty; a chemical delivery missed or late.
- Transfer pump tripped or its suction valve closed.
- A drain or sample valve on the day tank left open or passing.
- Level transmitter fault (compare with the sight glass).

## 5.4 Immediate checks
- Read the sight glass and compare it with the level transmitter.
- Walk the day tank bund: look for an open drain valve, a leak, or chemical in the bund.
- Check the bulk container level and the transfer pump status.
- Estimate the time to empty from the current level and the dosing rate.

## 5.5 Corrective actions
- Close any passing drain valve. Contain and neutralise spills following SOP-15.
- Transfer chemical by hand from a full container if the transfer pump has failed.
- Order an urgent delivery if bulk stock is below two days of use.
- Do not run a dosing pump dry; if the tank is empty, ask the control room to stop the pump and follow SOP-09 for
  turbidity control.

## 5.6 Escalation and records
- Inform the shift supervisor when any day tank is below the low alarm.
- Record deliveries, transfers and the cause of the low level in the shift log.
